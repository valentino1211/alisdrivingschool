Approved Driving Instructor

What the qualification actually means

‘ADI’ is the official DVSA qualification for driving instructors. It isn’t a marketing label — this is what it involves.

About instructors on GOV.UK (opens in a new tab)

  • Three DVSA qualifying tests

    Every ADI has passed a theory test, a test of their own driving, and a test of their ability to teach.

  • Background checked

    Anyone applying to become an instructor needs a Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS) check first.

  • Green badge in the windscreen

    Qualified instructors display a green badge; trainees display a pink one. Anyone you pay for lessons must be one or the other.

  • Regularly assessed

    ADIs usually take a DVSA standards check every 4 years, which assesses their ability to teach.

Learning to drive

Your road to a
full licence.

Six steps from provisional licence to driving on your own. Each one links to the official GOV.UK page, so you always know exactly what comes next.

  1. Step 1 / 6

    Get your provisional licence

    Apply to DVLA online. You can apply from 15 years and 9 months old, and most people can start driving a car at 17.

    • Apply from 15 years 9 months
    • Usually arrives within a week online
    Apply on GOV.UK (opens in a new tab)

  2. Step 2 / 6

    Start lessons with Ali

    Bring your provisional licence to your first lesson. You start somewhere quiet, get comfortable with the controls, and build from there at your own pace.

    • No experience needed
    • Motorway lessons available
    Book a lesson

  3. Step 3 / 6

    Prepare for the theory test

    The theory test has two parts: 50 multiple-choice questions in 57 minutes, then a hazard perception test of 14 video clips. You can start studying alongside your lessons.

    • 50 questions in 57 minutes
    • 14 hazard perception clips
    Free official practice (opens in a new tab)The Highway Code (opens in a new tab)

  4. Step 4 / 6

    Pass your theory test

    You need 43 out of 50 on the questions and 44 out of 75 on hazard perception. Your pass certificate lasts 2 years, and you need it before you can book the practical test.

    • Pass marks: 43/50 and 44/75
    • Valid for 2 years
    Book your theory test (opens in a new tab)

  5. Step 5 / 6

    Book your practical test

    You must now book, change or cancel your driving test yourself — your instructor isn’t allowed to. Talk to Ali first so the date fits your progress, and have his personal reference number to hand so the booking can check he’s free.

    • You book it yourself
    • Up to 2 changes allowed
    Book on GOV.UK (opens in a new tab)

  6. Step 6 / 6

    Pass — and drive on your own

    Expect around 35 minutes of driving: an eyesight check, two ‘show me, tell me’ questions, independent driving and possibly a reversing exercise. Pass with 15 or fewer driving faults and no serious or dangerous ones, and you can drive straight away.

    • About 35 minutes of driving
    • 15 or fewer driving faults
    What happens on the test (opens in a new tab)‘Show me, tell me’ questions (opens in a new tab)

Learner guide

Good to know,
before you set off.

Practical answers for learners and the family helping them — with official sources, checked in September 2026.

What to bring

  • Your provisional licence

    You need a valid provisional licence to drive on the road, so bring your photocard to every lesson.

  • Glasses or contact lenses, if you use them

    You must be able to read a number plate from 20 metres — about the length of five parked cars.

  • Comfortable shoes

    Flat shoes with a thin sole make it much easier to feel the pedals.

  • Nothing else

    No experience is needed. If you are nervous, say so — lessons go at your pace.

Source: GOV.UK eyesight rules (opens in a new tab)

Your supervisor must

  • Be over 21
  • Be qualified to drive the type of car you are learning in

  • Not be paid to supervise you
  • Not use a mobile phone while supervising

Insurance

You must be insured to drive the car as a learner. Some insurers require your supervisor to be over 25.

L plates

The car you practise in must display L plates.

No motorways

Not with family or friends: learners can only drive on a motorway with an Approved Driving Instructor, in a car with dual controls.

Ali's Driving School is run by Ali, an Approved Driving Instructor. Ali decides how any personal information you give him is used, which makes him responsible for it under UK data protection law. You can contact him by calling or texting 07985 409688.

What this website collects

Nothing. There are no accounts, no analytics, no adverts and no tracking, and the website doesn’t load anything from other companies: its fonts and pictures are its own.

The enquiry forms don’t send anything to the website either. When you press Text Ali, your own messages app opens with your text to Ali written out, so you can check it before you send it, and it goes straight to his phone. On a computer, the form can show a QR code to scan with your phone instead: the code is made on your computer, and nothing is sent anywhere.

The website is hosted on GitHub Pages, run by GitHub. Like most web hosts, GitHub records the IP address of each visit and keeps it for security purposes (see GitHub’s privacy statement (opens in a new tab)).

Cookies and cache

This website doesn’t use cookies.

  • The cookies and cache notice. When you press OK on it, your browser remembers that on your device (in its local storage, not a cookie) so the notice doesn’t keep coming back. It’s only a note that the notice was closed and when; it’s never sent anywhere, and after a year the notice shows again.
  • Your browser’s cache. Your browser keeps copies of this site’s pictures, fonts and code so it opens faster next time. The cache stays on your device, holds nothing about you, and you can clear it whenever you like in your browser’s settings (usually under “Clear browsing data”).
  • Other websites. Instagram, Facebook and GOV.UK have their own cookies and privacy policies, which apply once you follow a link to them. Nothing from them is built into this site, so they don’t see your visit here.

When you call or text Ali (including through the forms on this page), he receives the details you choose to give him. Usually that’s your name and your phone number (his phone shows him the number you call or text from), and it may include your area or postcode, your driving experience, when you’re usually free and anything else you write.

How your information is used

  • To reply to you and arrange lessons, because you’ve asked him to.

Ali never sells your information or uses it for marketing. It’s only shared when the law requires it (for example with HMRC, for tax) or when you ask him to share it.

Only for as long as it’s needed. An enquiry that doesn’t lead to lessons is deleted once it’s no longer needed. If you have lessons, your details are kept while you’re learning, and business records such as payments are kept for as long as the law requires.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you can ask Ali for a copy of the information he holds about you, ask him to correct it or delete it, ask him to limit how it’s used or to stop using it, and take back your permission for anything you agreed to, such as a pass photo. Just call or text him. It’s free, and he’ll reply within a month.
